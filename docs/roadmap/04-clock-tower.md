# Clock Tower (v0.5) — Vertical slice: one new biome end to end

[← Roadmap overview](../ROADMAP.md)


**Goal:** Build the Clock Tower end to end: terrain and about 6 telegraphed hazards, about 5 new monsters from the parts kit, a multi-phase boss on the BossScript core, candles that drop hearts for holy sub-weapons, rigged heroes and boss, and an organ and music-box score. The slice proves the pipeline and measures the real cost of each content unit.

**Demo moment:** You whip a candelabra in the Clock Tower, catch the heart and hurl a holy axe over a swinging pendulum blade while the gears grind, and the music box swells into the clock boss's organ theme.

**Why now:** The slice comes once the substrates exist, so nothing is built twice. It uses only the slices it needs of ENM-5 (BossScript core), AUD-7 (MusicDirector) and AUD-8 (one boss theme), and those epics complete in v0.7. Art is the long pole for every later content epic, so ART-3 rigs (absorbing CLS-8's pose swaps) and the ART-9 parts kit come before the next ~25 creatures. BLD-3 needs GEN-4's candles and UX-3's remappable action. BIO-3 absorbs GEN-5's traps. Measuring unit cost here is the only honest way to size v0.6–v1.0. Clock Tower art and music pre-production starts during v0.4.

**Estimate:** ~14 weeks

**Exit criteria:**

- [ ] The Clock Tower is playable as an opt-in route under an epoch-2 seed label, on the beta channel and opt-in on main. Existing seeds, seed history and dailies are untouched.
- [ ] Unit costs are measured and published (dev-days per monster, hazard, boss, room archetype, minute of music and SFX), and M5–M8 scope and weeks are re-baselined from them.
- [ ] A new monster goes from scaffold to merge in ≤ 2 dev-days using the parts kit. CI enforces the definition of done: canon name, t() strings, sfx block, provisional budget fields, codex stub and golden snapshot.
- [ ] The sub-weapon slot holds 8 IP-renamed sub-weapons with ammo from breakable candles, pre-rolled on the loot stream. It fits the UX-9 action set, and Item Crash becomes the CLS-1 ultimate rather than a new button.
- [ ] Water, pits and conveyor terrain plus about 6 hazard kinds ship with the safe-route solver proven by fuzz tests. Hazards damage monsters too, and a chandelier can be dropped on enemies.
- [ ] Heroes and the Clock Tower boss run on procedural rigs while crowds use pose swaps. The Clock Tower holds 60 FPS on the reference mid-range phone at Medium, with tick ≤ 4 ms in the CPU-throttled gate.
- [ ] Organ, harpsichord and music-box voices ship with phone fallbacks, and the Clock Tower theme is written in the score format and played by the Conductor.

#### GAP-6 · PRD-1 Clock Tower Vertical Slice and the 1.0 Roadmap

*Gaps found in review · effort XL · impact 5/5*

Turn the 104+ epics into gated milestones: Foundation-lite, Vertical Slice, 1.0 Content, 1.0 Hardening, then post-1.0. Every epic gets a place on a critical-path graph, a capacity estimate, a definition of done and a cut status. The slice is one new biome built end to end (Clock Tower) using only the slice of each foundation epic it needs. Players see something new early, and the cost of each content unit is measured rather than guessed. **Player value:** A new biome, boss and sub-weapons reach players months sooner than a foundations-first plan would allow. After that comes a 1.0 that actually ships, instead of 104 half-finished epics.

- **Machine-readable roadmap and critical path** — One JSON file records every epic's milestone, owner, size, dependencies, definition of done and cut status. A script computes the critical path, slack and capacity load per milestone.
  - *Implementation:* docs/roadmap/roadmap.json, validated against a schema. scripts/roadmap.ts prints the critical path, detects cycles and emits a code-drawn SVG Gantt chart as a CI artifact.
  - *Acceptance:* A count check confirms every epic ID in the plan is present. The graph has no cycles. The CI artifact shows the critical path and a load of at most 100% of declared capacity per milestone.
- **Foundation-lite gate (M0)** — The minimum shared work before the slice: GOV-1 contracts plus the migration week, TEC-1 CI, a minimal TEC-2 bus, TEC-4 save v2, GEN-1 per-pass seeds (queued for GOV-2's epoch), the BLD-1 Hit Ledger, a minimal ENM-1 behaviour set, BAL-1 rules, UX-6's t() function and the stream table. Each foundation epic declares a slice cut and defers everything else.
  - *Implementation:* A sliceCut field per foundation epic in roadmap.json, and a gate checklist in docs/roadmap/gates.md.
  - *Acceptance:* M0 exits once every listed slice cut has merged and CI has been green on main for 5 consecutive days. Nothing outside the slice cuts is needed to start M1.
- **Vertical Slice scope: Clock Tower (M1)** — One biome, end to end. Content: 4–5 new monsters built with ART-9 (for example a gear imp, pendulum knight, cuckoo harpy, bell wraith and a clockwork bat flock); one ENM-5 multi-phase boss under its DIR-1 canon name; BLD-3 heart-ammo sub-weapons on UX-9's slot; GEN-4 candle breakables that drop hearts; one BIO-3 hazard (rotating gear floors); an AUD-7 biome theme and an AUD-8 boss stinger. The first BIO-2 fork lets players choose Bone Catacombs or Clock Tower after Castle Gate. Codex entries, strings and BAL-3 balance bands are included.
  - *Implementation:* Clock Tower biome data in BIO-1 format, with all content behind the REL-1 flag biome.clockTower. The fork ships as part of the GOV-2 epoch-2 bundle.
  - *Acceptance:* The slice is playable from the title screen through the Clock Tower boss kill on phone and desktop, and passes ART-9's content definition of done. In the heaviest Clock Tower room, the mid-range reference phone on the Medium preset averages at least 60 FPS with a sim tick of 4 ms or less (in-game perf overlay). The slice adds at most 30 KB gzipped to the main bundle.
- **Per-unit content cost model** — Track actual effort for each slice unit (fodder monster, boss, hazard, theme, sub-weapon, codex page) by discipline: art, AI, audio, writing, balance and QA. From that, build a cost model with ±30% bands for sizing 1.0.
  - *Implementation:* An actuals block per unit in roadmap.json. scripts/costModel.ts computes medians and projects the cost of the 1.0 scope.
  - *Acceptance:* The model includes at least 4 monsters, 1 boss, 1 hazard and 1 theme. The 1.0 projection is published with its bands and reviewed at M1 exit.
- **1.0 scope and cut line** — Choose the 1.0 scope by measured capacity. Starting proposal: 6 biomes (the 3 originals uplifted, plus Clock Tower, Forbidden Library and Drowned Aqueduct), 9 bosses, 6 hunters, about 80 cards, one heat ladder, the codex, dailies, onboarding, English plus 2 languages, and the UX-2 and UX-4 accessibility sets. Post-1.0: SOC-6 co-op, SOC-8 mods, SOC-4 ghost races, a hosted SOC-5 board, the BIO-6 Tower of Trials, BIO-8 wave B and platform services bridges. Every cut item records a reason.
  - *Implementation:* A release field ('1.0', 'post' or 'cut') per epic in roadmap.json. The scope is reviewed at every gate.
  - *Acceptance:* The cost model's estimate for 1.0 is at most 1.2 × capacity. Every post and cut epic has a rationale string. The scope freezes at the M2 midpoint, after which any addition needs an equal cut.
- **Gate definitions of done and go/no-go playtests** — Each milestone has exit criteria: tests, a TEC-9 budget-ledger entry, extracted strings, accessibility checks, codex entries, flags, goldens and a UX-9 control audit. The slice ends with a go/no-go playtest of 12 external players split across phone and desktop.
  - *Implementation:* The criteria live in docs/roadmap/gates.md. The playtest protocol is shared with BAL-8, and results are recorded as an ADR.
  - *Acceptance:* A go needs a median fun rating of at least 4/5, at least 80% of players understanding the sub-weapon without help, no open S1 or S2 bugs, and a measured cost per monster within the planned budget. A no-go triggers a documented re-plan.

**Depends on:** GOV-1 Keystone Contracts; GOV-2 Epoch Two; ART-9 Monster Foundry; DIR-1 Crimson Canon; UX-9 Thumb Zone Charter; REL-1 Release Train (flags); TEC-9 Every Phone Survives (budget ledger); BIO-1, BIO-2, BIO-3, ENM-1, ENM-3, ENM-5, BLD-3, GEN-4, AUD-7, AUD-8 (slice cuts)
**Risks:** Scope creep on the slice ('just one more monster'). The slice list freezes at M0 exit.; Foundation owners may overbuild their slice cuts, so each cut has an explicit definition of done.; Learning-curve effort inflates the first measured unit costs. Discount the first unit and use medians.; Rushed slice work leaves tech debt. Reserve one hardening week inside M1.
**Constraint check:** Slice content obeys every hard constraint: code-drawn rigs, synthesized audio, and a deterministic biome covered by the epoch-2 corpus, within the sim budget and a 30 KB gzipped allowance. It is the first real check that a biome fits the 1 MB bundle and the 60 FPS mid-range phone target. The roadmap tooling is dev-only.

#### GAP-4 · ART-9 Monster Foundry: Ink Bible, parts kit, variants and one-command content intake

*Gaps found in review · effort L · impact 5/5*

Write the chunky-cartoon house style down as code and tests. Extract a rig-ready part library from the 13 existing rigs. Give every content type a one-command scaffolder and a definition of done that CI enforces, with all authoring tools in a single dev-only Keep Workshop shell. The 60–80 new creatures then become compositions of parts instead of 45-line hand drawings. **Player value:** Many more monsters, bosses, pets and NPCs arrive sooner, and all of them look like they belong to the same game as the reference. Silhouettes stay readable at a glance on a phone, and nothing drifts off-model.

- **Ink Bible as code** — Encode the style rules. Ink width per size class: fodder 3, elite 3.5, mini-boss 4 and boss 5 world units, scaled so on-screen strokes stay at least 1.5 px at render scale 0.55. Exactly one cel highlight per part group. Chibi head-to-body ratios of about 1:1.2 for heroes and 1:1.5 for fodder. Fixed eye-kit sizes. Per-biome palette ramps of base, shade and highlight token triplets. No red-dominant heroes, because red signals enemies and blood.
  - *Implementation:* src/render/art/bible.ts holds the constants and SizeClass. Pen gains a sizeClass and derives its outline width from it; pen.ts currently fixes OUTLINE = 3. tests/art.bible.test.ts walks the GraphicsContext instructions (pixi v8 context.instructions) to check stroke widths, highlight count and palette membership.
  - *Acceptance:* The test fails a rig that uses a colour outside the palette, has two highlights in one part group, or strokes outside its size class. All 13 existing rigs pass once the palette tokenization feature lands. DIR-1 rules on the Vanguard, which is crimson-dominant today.
- **Palette tokenization of existing art** — Replace the 116 raw hex literals (58 unique) in characters.ts, and those in props.ts, with THEME tokens and biome ramps. This unlocks ART-2 colourblind remaps, BIZ-1 blood-colour toggles and the variant generator below.
  - *Implementation:* theme.ts gains ramps and the missing tokens. Pen.part and Pen.shade accept a Tok. A codemod runs over src/render/art/*.ts.
  - *Acceptance:* Lint finds no 0xRRGGBB literals in src/render/art. TEC-1 golden screenshots of the class-select portraits and a combat room differ by no more than 0.5% of pixels.
- **Rig-ready parts kit** — Parametric parts: skulls, eyes, horns, wings, armor plates, robes and capes, tails, limbs and weapons. Each part has named anchors and pivots so ART-3 Marionette can animate it. The first parts are extracted from existing art: skull() from the skeleton and archer, the bat and gargoyle wings, the ogre horns, the Axe Armor plates, and the witch and Count robes.
  - *Implementation:* src/render/art/parts/{head,eyes,horns,wings,armor,robe,tail,limb,weapon}.ts, each with the signature part(p: Pen, anchor, size, ramp, variant). The rigs in characters.ts are rewritten as compositions, and each rig descriptor exports its anchor tree for ART-3.
  - *Acceptance:* The 13 existing rigs rebuilt from the kit match their goldens within a 1% pixel difference. The three new fodder monsters built for the PRD-1 slice average no more than 25 lines of rig code each, counted by script.
- **Deterministic variant generator** — Palette and part swaps for elites, biome variants (for example a frost Bone Archer for the water biome) and ENM-2 affix looks. Variants are seeded by content ID, never by the run seed. Each is built once, cached, and gets a matching flash (silhouette) context automatically.
  - *Implementation:* src/render/art/variants.ts generates them. shapes.ts registers variants lazily per biome on room load and caches them in a Map<variantId, CharShapes>. The budget is at most 400 resident GraphicsContexts.
  - *Acceptance:* A counter test confirms each variant context is built exactly once. Loading every biome stays within the resident-context budget, and every variant has a flash context.
- **Silhouette distinctness and readability gate** — Render each rig's white flash context to a 64×64 mask, and require IoU below 0.75 between any two enemies on the same biome roster. Also check minimum feature size (eyes, weapon tips) at render scale 0.55.
  - *Implementation:* e2e/art.silhouette.spec.ts, part of the TEC-1 Playwright job, calls renderer.extract.pixels on each flash context. Roster data comes from the BIO-1 biome tables, and results go into an HTML report artifact.
  - *Acceptance:* A deliberately cloned rig fails the gate. The full roster runs in under 60 s. The report lists the closest pairs in each biome.
- **One-command content scaffolder** — `npm run new:monster <key>` (and new:boss, new:card, new:event, new:biome) generates every piece a content unit needs: the registry ID from GOV-1, the enemyDefs row, a rig stub assembled from chosen parts, flash registration, a ZzFX sfx block, codex and bestiary stubs, t() string keys (UX-6), AttackIds, a BAL-3 budget row and a smoke test.
  - *Implementation:* scripts/scaffold/*.ts, Node-only with templates and no runtime dependencies. It writes into src/content/*, src/render/art/rigs/*, src/audio/sfxDefs.ts and strings/en.json.
  - *Acceptance:* After scaffolding a monster, npm test passes immediately. The new monster spawns in the Workshop sandbox, and git diff shows only the expected files.
- **Keep Workshop dev shell** — A single ?dev shell hosts the Art Lab (ART-8), Audio Lab (AUD-5), prefab sandbox (BIO-4), tuning console (BAL-8), event and story editor (STO-8), spawn-table viewer and touch heatmap (UX-9). It has one router, one flag, one chunk policy, and typed data tables with hand-written validators and Vite HMR hot reload.
  - *Implementation:* src/dev/workshop.ts defines a tool registry interface {id, mount, unmount}. It loads by dynamic import only when import.meta.env.DEV or a staging-mode flag is set. src/content/schema.ts holds the validators, hand-written instead of zod to keep the bundle small.
  - *Acceptance:* A bundle test confirms the production chunks contain no workshop code. Every listed tool registers through the interface. Editing a data table hot-reloads it without losing the current room.
- **Content definition of done in CI** — For each content type (monster, boss, card, item, event, biome, pet, NPC), CI enforces that these all exist: data, rig and flash context, a silhouette pass, SFX, a codex entry, strings in every shipped language (or a flagged fallback), a balance band and a smoke test. Work-in-progress content is allowed only behind a REL-1 feature flag.
  - *Implementation:* tests/content.checklist.test.ts iterates the src/content/ids.ts registries and checks the cross-references.
  - *Acceptance:* Removing the codex entry of any shipped monster fails CI, while flagged-off WIP content passes. The checklist output is attached to every release.

**Depends on:** GOV-1 Keystone Contracts (content-ID registry); ART-8 Art Lab; ART-3 Marionette (anchor trees); ART-2 Read the Room (token remap); TEC-1 Night Watch CI (Playwright, goldens); UX-6 Tongues of the Keep (t()); BAL-3 The Ledger; DIR-1 Crimson Canon (palette rulings, names); REL-1 Release Train (flags)
**Risks:** A parts kit can make monsters look samey, so the Bible requires at least one bespoke silhouette feature per creature.; Rebuilding existing rigs from parts may shift their look slightly. Goldens plus an art-director review catch it.; Scaffolder templates rot as the contracts change, so tests cover the template output.; The silhouette IoU threshold needs tuning to avoid false positives on deliberately related variants, which go on an allowlist.
**Constraint check:** Everything stays vector code drawn through Pen into GraphicsContexts that are built once and shared. Variants are cached under a capped budget. The Workshop and scaffolders never ship in production. Shared parts should shrink the per-monster bundle cost compared with bespoke 45-line rigs. Art is presentation-only, so determinism is unaffected. Stroke widths per size class keep rigs readable from render scale 0.55 on phones up to 4K.

#### ART-3 · 3. MARIONETTE: Procedural rig animation, gaits and facial expressions

*Art, animation, VFX & lighting · effort XL · impact 5/5*

Turn every chibi from a stiff cut-out into a puppet. Split the existing vector art at the joints and drive it with procedural walk cycles, anticipation and follow-through, flinches, springy capes and plumes, and big expressive faces that blink, glare and track you. **Player value:** Characters feel alive. Telegraphs are carried by body language: the Bone Archer visibly draws, the Axe Armor rears back before charging, and Gorehorn's heads bicker. The reference's 'cute but chunky' charm comes through in motion, and personality makes later content (pets, new bosses) much cheaper to bring to life.

- **Rig data model and part extraction** — A RigDef describes a character as parts (head, torso, armF, armB, legF, legB, weapon, accessory such as cape, plume, scarf, ponytail or wings), each with a parent, pivot and z-order. Existing draw functions are split along the joints with the same coordinates, so the assembled rest pose matches today's art.
  - *Implementation:* New src/render/rig/rig.ts (RigDef, RigInstance), src/render/rig/parts.ts (part draw functions such as drawVanguardHead, drawVanguardLegF, drawSkeletonSkull, drawSkeletonRibs and drawSkeletonFemur, refactored out of src/render/art/characters.ts) and src/render/rig/poses.ts. Each part is built via both() to get 'n' and 'w' contexts, so hit flash still works per part. Joints get overlap caps (a small circle in the parent's color) to hide outline seams. RigInstance is a pooled Container with one Graphics per part. ShapeCache gains rigs: Record<string, RigDef>.
  - *Acceptance:* Golden bounds test: for all 3 heroes, 7 enemies and 3 bosses, the rest-pose rig's union bounds match the current monolithic shape within 1 px using the recording-context hasher from Epic 8. ShapeCache build stays under 60 ms on desktop and under 150 ms on a mid phone. The flash silhouette covers every part.
- **Distance-driven procedural locomotion and gaits** — Legs swing +/-0.5 rad with foot lift and contact squash, arms counter-swing +/-0.35 rad, the torso bobs 2.5 px at twice the stride frequency and the head lags on a spring. Each archetype has its own gait. Skeleton: rattle-walk with 0.08 rad jitter and jaw chatter at 8 Hz. Ghoul: a lurching shamble with a 0.3 phase skew and a dragged leg. Axe Armor: a heavy 1.2 Hz stomp with a 4 px dust puff and a 0.5 micro-shake within 300 px. Blood Bat: +/-0.9 rad wing beats at 16 Hz with a body counter-bob. Hex Witch: hover sway with rippling robe tatters. Gargoyle: wings fold on landing. Player stride is speed-matched to the AGI-scaled move speed (26 px stride) so feet never skate.
  - *Implementation:* New src/render/rig/locomotion.ts with a GAIT table keyed by ENEMY_DEFS.key and hero ClassId. E.phase is plain time, so the renderer keeps a per-slot Float32Array distPhase accumulated from interpolated position deltas (heroes reuse p.walkPhase, which is already distance-based in playerCombat.ts). The pose is written into the rig's Float32Array pose buffer (rot, dx, dy, sx, sy per part).
  - *Acceptance:* Foot-skate test (headless evaluator): during the contact phase, foot world velocity is below 15% of body speed for every gait at 0.5x, 1x and 1.5x speed. All 10 monster rigs and 3 heroes have GAIT entries (exhaustiveness test). At zero velocity, poses return to the idle breath cycle within 0.2 s.
- **Anticipation, action and follow-through clips synced to sim timers** — Code-defined keyframe clips with easing. Vanguard arc slash: 0.06 s pull-back to -1.6 rad with a backward lean, a 0.05 s strike to +1.4 rad with smear, overshoot to +1.7, then a 0.11 s settle, summing to swingT 0.22. Weaver cast: staff raise with a hair whip. Phantom: alternating left and right dagger jabs with a scarf whip. Bone Archer: draw and loose over the 0.6 s windup. Hex Witch: arms raise and the hat tilts. Axe Armor: axe overhead, then lean forward 0.35 rad into the charge. Gorehorn: club overhead and a belly squash to 0.82 on landing. Rune Colossus: a fist slam for spikes. Count: a cape sweep for hellfire.
  - *Implementation:* New src/render/rig/actions.ts: clips are small const arrays [t, partIndex, rot, dx, dy] with easing ids (easeOutBack, easeInQuad, easeOutElastic) and are time-remapped to sim timers (p.swingT, p.castT, E.state plus E.t), so the animation never contradicts hit timing. The clip is selected from (def key, E.state). Bosses map their numbered states (ogre 1/2/4/5, golem 4-7, count 4-7) explicitly.
  - *Acceptance:* For every attack clip, the 'strike' key aligns with the sim damage or emit tick within 1 rendered frame (a test samples the clip at the sim event time for each enemy and boss state). The clip table covers every (key, attack state) pair the AI uses (exhaustiveness test against enemyAi state constants).
- **Hurt, stagger and per-part death on the rig** — A flinch snaps the head back 0.4 rad and leans the torso along the knockback. Elites stagger for 0.25 s. Deaths become per-part: the Skeleton separates into skull, ribs and femurs; the Axe Armor into helmet, pauldrons and axe; the Gargoyle into wing shards. Parts fly on ballistic arcs and settle, feeding the Epic 1 corpse pool. The player's kneel-and-collapse is re-authored as a rig clip.
  - *Implementation:* Add hurt and stagger clips in src/render/rig/actions.ts, driven by Epic 1's FxSink.impact. A death converter in src/render/corpses.ts takes a RigInstance's current pose and turns each part into a corpse debris entry (shared context, pooled), using render-PRNG arcs.
  - *Acceptance:* Death debris is 8 parts or fewer per corpse and settles within 1.2 s. The flinch is visible within 1 frame of the impact event. Corpse pool caps from Epic 1 still hold with per-part debris.
- **Facial expression system and gaze tracking** — Faces get separate eye parts (sclera, pupil, glint), brows and mouth variants (neutral, grin, grit, 'O', frown, fang-smirk). Pupils track the aim target (heroes) or the player (monsters), clamped inside the sclera, and characters blink every 2.5-5 s. Hero expressions: grit when attacking, '>_<' when hurt, a sweat drop and worried brows below 30% HP, star eyes on level-up. Enemy expressions: angry brows on windup, '@ @' spiral eyes when stunned, a smug smirk from Vlador when he teleports, an angry left head and a dopey right head on Gorehorn, and a Colossus eye slit that narrows to 2 px on enrage.
  - *Implementation:* Refactor Pen.eye into drawEyeParts (sclera, pupil and glint contexts) in src/render/pen.ts. New src/render/rig/face.ts: an expression enum, a per-instance expression state in Uint8Array, and blink timers from the render PRNG seeded by slot and room. Mouth and brow variants are prebuilt contexts swapped via Graphics.context. Pupil offset is computed from the look vector and clamped to (scleraR - pupilR).
  - *Acceptance:* A unit test shows the pupil never leaves the sclera for any look vector. Expression changes take effect within 1 frame of the trigger (hurt, windup, stun, low HP). Every hero has all 6 expressions and every boss at least 4. Blinks occur 12-24 times per minute per character.
- **Secondary motion springs (plumes, capes, scarves, hair)** — The Vanguard's red crest plume, the Weaver's white ponytail, the Phantom's crimson scarf tail, the Count's cape (3 segments that billow on teleport), the Hex Witch's hat tip and the bat ears all trail with inertia and settle after dashes and knockbacks.
  - *Implementation:* New src/render/rig/springs.ts: SoA angular spring chains of 2-4 segments per accessory, driven by root acceleration from interpolated positions. Each segment is a separate static part context rotated about its pivot, so nothing is re-tessellated per frame. Chains are disabled on the Low preset and for off-screen rigs.
  - *Acceptance:* Zero allocation over 10k frames. Spring update for 300 rig instances takes under 0.4 ms on desktop. On Low, accessories hold the rest pose. A dash produces visible trailing of at least 0.3 rad that settles within 0.4 s.
- **Boss rigs with personality** — Gorehorn: two independent heads that look at each other and bicker at idle (mouths alternate 'O'), the struck head glares at you, and a two-joint club arm. Rune Colossus: floating boulder fists hovering on a 6 px sine, a sliding head block and a slowly rotating rune spiral. Count Vlador: his cape opens like bat wings (1.6x) for bat summons, his collar flares, and his eyes glint before he teleports.
  - *Implementation:* Add boss RigDefs in src/render/rig/parts.ts with the existing extraA/extraB parts (ogreClub, golemRunes, golemCracks) folded into the rigs. Add boss pose sets in src/render/rig/poses.ts keyed by the boss state numbers from enemyAi.ts.
  - *Acceptance:* Each boss has at least 6 named poses (idle, one windup per attack, recover, enrage, hurt, death) in the Art Lab scrubber. Gorehorn's heads never face the same way for more than 3 s at idle. The boss rig evaluates in under 0.05 ms.
- **Pose evaluator budget and LOD fallback** — One evaluator updates every visible rig per render frame from slot-indexed Float32Array pose buffers. Off-screen rigs are skipped. Small enemies fall back to baked pose frames from Epic 4 when more than the crowd threshold are visible or on Low.
  - *Implementation:* Add RigSystem in src/render/rig/rigSystem.ts, owned by GameRenderer. It replaces the bodyWrap-only logic in syncEnemies/syncPlayer while keeping the same view culling (viewL/R/T/B). Its LOD switch selects the vector rig or the baked Sprite path from Epic 4.
  - *Acceptance:* A headless (Pixi-free) benchmark evaluates 400 rigs x 8 parts in under 0.5 ms on desktop and under 1.5 ms on mid-phone emulation. Heap is flat over 10k frames. LOD switching is invisible at 1x zoom (Art Lab A/B).

**Depends on:** ART Epic 4 (Baked Goods): baked pose atlases for crowd LOD and the Low preset; ART Epic 8 (Art Lab): the rig scrubber and recording-context hasher are needed for authoring; build its gallery in parallel with this epic; COMBAT/AI: stable, named state constants per boss (export them from enemyAi.ts instead of magic numbers 1-7), so clips can map to them safely; AUDIO: footstep, bone-chatter and armor-stomp SFX synced to gait contact events
**Risks:** Draw-object count multiplies (8-10 parts x 140 enemies). Mitigated by the LOD fallback to baked sprites and by culling.; Ink outlines may show seams at joints. Mitigated by joint caps, back-to-front z-order and overlap tests in the Art Lab.; Rest-pose drift from the beloved current art. Mitigated by the golden bounds tests and side-by-side comparison.; Animation could contradict hit timing. #### AUD-5 · Grimoire: a tiny tracker-style score format and Conductor

Music never hiccups between rooms, and each run varies subtly (fills, trills, arpeggio shapes) so the 200th Castle Gate room doesn't sound like a loop.

  - *Implementation:* src/audio/score/compile.ts compiles lazily per floor during the room transition.
  - *Acceptance:* Compiling all songs takes under 5 ms. Playback allocates nothing (the Conductor only reads the arrays). Each song's compiled table is snapshot-tested.
- **Conductor with a musical clock** — Supports sections (intro, A, B, bridge, outro), loop points, queue(section, quantize: 'beat'|'bar'|'phrase'), onBar/onBeat callbacks, tempo ramps (accelerando on enrage) and per-song meters. It schedules through Epic 1's clock and channel strips.

  - *Acceptance:* The same seed gives an identical scheduled-event hash over 64 bars, and a new attempt changes the fill sequence. Returning to explore after a battle resumes at the saved bar (±1 bar for quantization).

  - *Acceptance:* Muting L3 removes every drum event from the schedule log, not just its gain. A stem fade-in from 0 to 1 completes in exactly one bar.
- **Audio Lab (dev only)** — ?audiolab=1 opens a song list with section buttons, an intensity slider, stem mute and solo, an SFX browser with category filter and caption preview, and live peak/RMS meters.
  - *Implementation:* src/dev/audioLab.ts is loaded by a dynamic import behind the URL flag. Vite splits it into its own chunk, and vite.config.ts PWA globIgnores keeps it out of the precache.
  - *Acceptance:* With the lab in the codebase, the main bundle size changes by ≤0.5 KB, and the lab works offline in dev.
